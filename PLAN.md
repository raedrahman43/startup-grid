# Startup Grid — product plan

## Idea and audience
A daily 3×3 trivia game for startup founders, operators, investors, and tech readers. Name a company that satisfies both intersecting clues. Nine guesses; each company can appear once. Share results without spoilers.

## Prototype
Playable puzzle, searchable answers, match validation, duplicate protection, remaining guesses, browser-local progress, answer review, result copying, directory, sponsorship inventory, and DM pitch preparation. Nine real companies now use dated primary-source funding announcements; each directory card links to evidence. Multiple eligible answers per cell are accepted. Payments, message delivery, live submissions, analytics, and daily rotation are not connected.

## Editorial model
Question families include funding raised, product line, named investors, notable talent, location, accelerators, and documented controversies. Each fact needs a company ID, predicate, value, source URL, source date, checked date, editor, and correction history. Funding facts specify currency, date, equity/debt, and announced/closed amounts. Talent clues specify role and time period. Freeze a fact snapshot per puzzle.

Controversy clues should describe specific dated events, distinguish allegations from findings, cite reliable documentation, and receive editorial review. Provide corrections and company responses. Never invent real-company claims. Sponsorship does not influence editorial eligibility or scoring.

## Revenue hypothesis
Sell a labeled presenting sponsor, startup spotlight, and directory placement. Begin with manually negotiated pilot packages. Set prices after measuring audience, clicks, and qualified inquiries; no invented traffic claims or guaranteed leads. Keep paid placements visually separate from game answers.

## DM listing funnel
“DM us to get your startup added” opens the actual brand social profile after setup. Prepare a message with company name, URL, product description, funding/investor source links, and contact. Separate editorial listing requests from advertising inquiries. Requests require review; payment does not guarantee inclusion in a puzzle.

## Delivery phases
1. Test prototype with five founders and five trivia players. Check clue comprehension, mobile usability, completion, and share intent.
2. Choose brand and actual DM destination. Check name/domain availability independently. Curate source-backed companies and seven reviewed puzzles.
3. Add company/fact/puzzle/sponsor tables and an authenticated editorial dashboard, corrections queue, and sponsor calendar.
4. Publish dated puzzle snapshots in a defined timezone. Validate guesses on the server so public code does not expose answers. Prove every board has a nine-company solution before release.
5. Connect inquiries, social profile, consent-aware analytics, and manually fulfilled sponsorships. Add payments after packages are defined.
6. Launch a small pilot. Track completion, returning players, shares, listing requests, sponsor clicks, and qualified inquiries.

## Acceptance criteria
Every intersection has eligible answers and the whole board has a unique-company solution. Wrong guesses cost an attempt. Duplicates are blocked. Completed answers cannot change. Results share without spoilers. Dialogs work with keyboard. Grid fits mobile. Ads and historical data are labeled. No submission is represented as delivered without delivery.

## Open decisions
Final name, target audience, DM profile, difficulty, mature former startup eligibility, launch timezone, sponsor packages, and editorial owner.
